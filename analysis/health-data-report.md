# Withings and Hevy analysis

Prepared 27 September 2026. Body measurements and Hevy workouts end 26 September; steps and sleep include 27 September. September is a partial month.

## Main finding

There are clear, sustained periods of estimated fat loss and regain, but **no consistent relationship showing that more logged strength training, more steps, or longer recorded sleep reliably predicts your fat loss**. Recent declining steps are a plausible contributor worth investigating, not an established explanation. You confirmed that Hevy contains all workouts for the last few years, that the main change was probably dietary, and that you started statins approximately within the past twelve months, with the date uncertain. This makes dietary changes a leading explanation to investigate, but intake is not measured in these exports. Statin timing and effects cannot be assessed from the available record.

## What coincides with losing or gaining fat?

### Strength training: more sessions did not consistently mean more fat loss

Hevy contains 232 workouts and 4,467 logged sets: 38 workouts in 2023, 70 in 2024, 69 in 2025 and 53 so far in 2026, plus two in December 2022. All exported sets are labelled normal. Typical session duration was 46 minutes.

- You gained approximately 3.16 kg of estimated fat between January and December 2024 despite logging 70 workouts that year.
- During the recent loss phase, October 2025–March 2026, you logged 26 workouts. During April–26 September 2026, when fat was generally rising, you logged 38. These calendar windows have similar lengths but the body changes above compare monthly averages.
- December 2025 and March 2026 contain no Hevy sessions. Your confirmation that all workouts are logged supports treating these as genuine workout gaps. Their low fat measurements cannot establish that stopping training caused fat loss: diet changes, illness or other circumstances could explain both.
- A simple monthly comparison actually associates more sessions with more fat gain (r = +0.36 across 45 months from January 2023). That relationship weakens substantially when changing the analysis window and does not predict subsequent change. It is not evidence that lifting causes fat gain.

For the primary weekly comparison, logged sessions versus the following four-week fat change had **r = −0.03 across 144 eligible observations**. Training volume (sum of load × reps) was essentially uncorrelated, r = −0.001. Sets and duration were also near zero. In 35 nonoverlapping 28-day blocks, sessions had r = +0.14 with within-block fat change. These inconsistent, small associations do not identify a fat-loss training dose.

Training volume also becomes less comparable when exercises change: a kilogram on one machine, a barbell exercise and a bodyweight exercise do not represent equivalent work. RPE is not used to infer effort. None of these findings measures training's effects on strength, function or health.

### Walking: recent decline is a useful lead, but not a dependable historical predictor

Recorded steps averaged about **10,668/day in February–May 2026**, versus **5,991/day in June–26 September**: a decline of about 44%. September through the 27th averaged about 4,295/day. Fat rose during the later period while strength training continued.

However, March–May already showed an estimated fat increase from 17.79 to 19.60 kg, even as recorded steps rose from about 8,915/day to 12,660/day. Thus the regain began before the big step reduction. Likewise, the October–December 2025 fat loss occurred without consistently high steps.

Across eligible weeks, steps versus following four-week fat change had **r = +0.13 (n = 125)**; in nonoverlapping blocks, **r = −0.07 (n = 31)**. Neither establishes a dependable association. Before 2026, the block correlation was +0.07, so a hidden strong negative relationship is not apparent in the earlier data either.

The early-2026 step jump also follows a device association dated 22 January 2026. The device file does not establish the device type or prove the cause of the jump. This is a reason to verify tracking consistency, not to discard those steps. Missing step data are substantial in parts of 2024 and August–October 2025, including all September 2025; missing days are not treated as zero.

### Sleep: a counterintuitive association that should not guide sleep reduction

Recorded main sleep averaged about **6.26 hours during October 2025–March 2026**, versus **6.86 hours during April–26 September 2026**. The fat-loss period therefore coincided with shorter recorded sleep.

Monthly and nonoverlapping-block analyses show a modest positive association between sleep duration and fat gain (r ≈ +0.33), but sleep in a given week barely predicts following four-week change (r = +0.09, n = 142). An exploratory bootstrap interval for the block association included zero. This could reflect shared changes in routine or health, recording differences, or chance. **It does not show that less sleep helps fat loss.**

Average sleeping heart rate and recorded awake time also showed little relationship with following four-week fat change (r = +0.02 and −0.07 respectively). Sleep episodes may include naps, split nights and overlapping device records; the longest episode per waking date was used, so the sleep measure is a proxy for main sleep rather than verified total daily sleep.

## Measurement noise is an important finding

Of 735 weight rows, 658 contain fat mass. Aggregating repeated measurements leaves 719 weight days and 650 fat-measurement days. Approximately 99% of measurements occurred before noon, which helps consistency.

For successive measurement days no more than three days apart:

- Median absolute change in estimated fat was **0.31 kg**.
- The 90th percentile was **0.83 kg** and the 95th percentile about **1.01 kg**.
- Estimated fat change and water change were inversely correlated, **r = −0.67**.

Fat and water estimates come from the same scale model, so this is not an independent physiological discovery. It is consistent with substantial short-term measurement sensitivity. Withings confirms hydration and measurement conditions influence body composition results. The multi-month changes are more persuasive than an isolated rise after a workout. [Withings measurement guidance](https://support.withings.com/hc/en-us/articles/218970667-Body-Body-Composition-Accuracy-Issues).

## Practical interpretation

1. **Reconstruct the dietary changes around November 2025 and April 2026.** Your recollection makes this the most useful next lead. If available, the statin prescription or dispensing date would help locate medication initiation on the timeline, although timing alone would not establish an effect. The data do not support attributing the changes to statins or changing treatment on that basis.
2. **Track food and drink alongside your existing measurements for several weeks**, including workout versus rest days and weekends. This would allow testing whether appetite or intake changes accompany training and whether intake remained similar as walking fell. Energy intake and activity both matter for weight management. [NIDDK guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/adult-overweight-obesity/eating-physical-activity).
3. **Treat restoring consistent walking as a hypothesis to test**, especially given the recent decline, without assuming a particular step target guarantees fat loss. Keep tracking-device use consistent.
4. **Judge progress by multi-week weight and body-fat trends**, supported by a consistently measured waist circumference. Avoid changing your plan based on a single estimated-fat reading. Continue consistent measurement conditions; Withings recommends weighing at the same time, ideally shortly after waking.
5. **Do not reduce strength training or sleep because of the positive contemporaneous correlations.** This observational record cannot isolate their causal effects.

## Method and limitations

Sources: `Withings/weight.csv`, `sleep.csv`, `aggregates_steps.csv`, `activities.csv`, `devices.csv`, and `Hevy/workouts.csv`. The export README establishes units. No source files were changed. Body-fat observations extend January 2022–September 2026, but early data are sparse, including no fat readings May–September 2022. Most behaviour comparisons use late 2022 onward.

- Withings offset timestamps were converted to Australia/Perth; naive weight and Hevy timestamps were assumed local. Workouts were grouped by start timestamp to avoid counting exercise sets as separate sessions. All 97 Withings weight-training activities matched Hevy workout dates, so they were not added as extra sessions. Your confirmation of complete workout logging supports using zero on days without a Hevy workout; these days may still include walking and other incidental activity.
- Daily body measurements were reduced to medians. Sunday-ending weekly means required at least two fat-measurement days; steps required five observed days and sleep four. Training summaries required a full seven-day interval inside Hevy coverage.
- Primary correlations compare exposure in a week with the difference between that week's mean fat mass and the mean four weeks later. One-week changes, rank correlations, monthly comparisons and nonoverlapping blocks were sensitivity checks. Overlapping future windows are not independent observations; no significance claims are made from their nominal sample sizes.
- Nonoverlapping 28-day blocks begin 2 January 2023. Fat change compares the median in the last seven days with the first seven, requiring two fat days at both ends. Steps/sleep require 20 recorded days; training requires coverage throughout. Only 35 blocks qualified, and only four were in 2026, limiting sensitivity to recent changes. Exploratory resampling intervals for steps, sessions, sets and sleep all crossed zero; resampling does not eliminate residual time dependence or confounding.
- Monthly levels are descriptive. Sampling frequency varies, September 2026 is incomplete, and April 2026 has only four fat-measurement days. The headline paired endpoints have 8–20 days each.
- Correlations use **changes** in fat rather than just fat levels, reducing misleading associations between unrelated long-term trends. They are exploratory, involve several comparisons and cannot establish causation or absence of benefit. No multivariable causal model is justified by these data.
- Device-derived calorie expenditure is not food intake and was not interpreted as energy balance. No reliable nutrition, alcohol, medication or illness log was available. Absolute body-fat accuracy is not validated against an independent reference.
